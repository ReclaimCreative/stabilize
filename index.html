Abstract

A Solution for Artists with Essential Tremor

Essential Tremor affects more than 10 million people in the United States. It is characterized by a rhythmic shaking, most commonly in the hands and forearms. For people living with essential tremor, fine motor skills—like those used when drawing—are compromised. For artists, this represents a significant decline in quality of life, since the base function of self expression is undermined.

Reclaim Creative is a proposal to use a combination of robotics and digital signal processing to filter out essential tremor and allow an artist to regain their ability to draw. The system comprises three components: a stylus, a surface outfitted with position sensors, and a Cartesian-style plotting robot capable of illustrating on a 2-D surface.

The stylus is held by the artist inside a sensor zone where time-of-flight sensors triangulate its position. The triangulated signal is filtered to remove tremor content, and the cleaned signal drives the Cartesian plotter to faithfully reproduce the artist's intended motion.

The role of artistic expression to human happiness is paramount; our goal is not simply to help people live their lives, but to provide them with more access to the things that make life worth living.

Design Details

System Architecture & Engineering

Cartesian Plotter
Full Plotter Assembly
Five pieces of aluminum V-slot extrusion form the frame. Stationary GT2 belts run the length of each rail, contacting the motor shaft wheels for self-tensioning. Two NEMA 17 steppers drive the Y-axis gantries; one drives the X-axis carriage. TMC2209 stepper drivers provide overcurrent detection and silent operation. The system achieves 80 steps/mm positional accuracy with negligible drift over time.
Y-Axis Gantry Plate
Laser-cut gantry plate with four V-wheels engaging the 2040 extrusion rails, providing smooth, low-friction travel along the Y axis.
Pen Lifting Mechanism
Spring-loaded, servo-actuated pen lifter. Neutral state is pen-down; the servo presses upward to lift when no pen-down command is received, protecting the drawing surface during repositioning.
The Stylus
Stylus Cross-Section
Pressing the actuating tip creates a momentary circuit closure via mechanical switch, powering the IR LED at the opposite end. The plotter reads this infrared signal as the pen-down command, enabling precise on/off control without any wireless communication.
Stylus Circuit
Minimal circuit: a 1.5 V AA cell, mechanical switch, 10Ω current-limiting resistor, and infrared LED. No microcontroller required in the stylus itself.
Sensor Array & Position Triangulation
Two VL53L7CX time-of-flight sensors placed a known distance apart on the left side of the active field. The law of cosines yields X/Y Cartesian coordinates from the two measured ranges R1 and R2. Each sensor uses a Single-Photon Avalanche Diode (SPAD) array with multi-zone ranging capability. A Nucleo-L476RG microcontroller reads both sensors and transmits filtered coordinates over USART.
Signal Processing & Control
Signal Processing Pipeline
Raw X and Y position data from the sensor array feeds into an FIR low-pass filter block. The filtered coordinates are then algebraically remapped to discrete stepper-motor steps. Sampling occurs at 60 Hz—well above the Nyquist rate for typical tremor frequencies (5–12 Hz). The pipeline spans three isolated microcontrollers: a Nucleo-L476RG handles sensor reading and USART transmission; a Nucleo-G474RE runs the Simulink motor-control model; an Arduino manages pen-lift logic via hobby servo, fully isolated from the main power tree.
Future Improvements
Tunable Filter
Expose filter cutoff frequency as a user-adjustable parameter to accommodate the full clinical range of tremor (3–18 Hz) rather than a fixed band.
Multi-Input Mode Switching
Add software mode-switching to accept multiple UART input sources, enabling future sensor or control-surface upgrades without hardware redesign.
Gantry Stiffness
Increase cross-beam rigidity to eliminate racking under rapid direction changes, improving accuracy at higher drawing speeds.
User Feedback LCD
Integrate a small LCD panel to display system status, filter mode, and sensor signal quality—reducing reliance on a connected computer during use.
Technical Details

Mathematics & Implementation

Plotter Homing State Machine
Plotter Homing Sequence
On power-up the plotter runs a two-axis homing sequence before accepting plot commands. The X axis seeks its limit switch, backs off, then the Y axis repeats the same procedure using both left and right home switches simultaneously. Once both axes are zeroed, the machine enters Ready and waits for a start_plot signal. Any e-stop event transitions immediately to Fault, which resets to Boot after 15 seconds.

Sensor A and Sensor B each read a median zone distance from their 4×4 SPAD array. The two distances dₐ and dₑ and the fixed baseline c fully define the triangle; the law of cosines yields angle α at Sensor A, giving Cartesian coordinates directly.

UART Serial Protocol
Sensor MCU → Plotter MCU (USART3)
Baud rate:  115200
Frame:     8N1
Packet size: 10 bytes
Packet structure
0xAA  [start byte]
bytes 1–4  x coordinate (float, 32-bit)
bytes 5–8  y coordinate (float, 32-bit)
0xFF  [end byte]
x and y are IEEE 754 single-precision floats transmitted little-endian via memcpy. They carry the already-computed Cartesian coordinates in mm, derived from the law of cosines on the sensor MCU before transmission.
